**A major, unplanned finding while verifying the entry mechanism, sharper than the edge expectation above: the entry condition itself (RSI(14)<30 AND close>SMA(50)) may be far rarer than the rule's own framing assumes - possibly to the point of almost never firing on real daily bars for liquid large-caps.** Exhaustively checked two independent ways, both converging on the same answer: (1) real data - scanned 1000 days of real daily bars across 18 liquid, actively-traded symbols (the 12-symbol watchlist plus AVGO/PLTR/SMCI/AMD/CRM/SHOP for a wider, more volatile net); RSI(14)<30 occurred on 436 separate days, and price was above SMA(50) on **zero** of them (closest miss: DIS, off by $6.09). (2) Synthetic construction - hundreds of thousands of hand-parametrized and randomly-searched synthetic price paths (smooth uptrends, choppy uptrends, old-dip-then-recovery, front-loaded crashes, plateau-then-dip, single-day crashes) found zero qualifying cases either; a genuinely qualifying fixture was only found via direct numerical optimization against the real `rsi()`/`sma()` functions (RSI=29.56, close $0.44 above SMA(50) - see the new test in `lib/db/bot-runs.test.ts`), and it required a specific, narrow combination the manual searches never landed on. The likely mechanism: by the time a 14-period Wilder RSI on a liquid stock genuinely crosses below 30, its 50-day simple average has almost always already been dragged below the current price too - "oversold within an uptrend" is a real, nameable pattern in trader lore, but this exact, precise operationalization of it (RSI(14) Wilder + SMA(50) simple, daily closes) may be stricter than that mental model. **This is a rule-design question, not an implementation bug** - the code correctly implements the rule as specified; the question is whether the rule as specified will ever have a real chance to fire.

**Follow-up investigation (no code changed - `lib/trading/bot-rule.ts` is still exactly as shipped): the same 1000-day/18-symbol scan, re-run for four candidate parameter variants, to see whether a small adjustment gives the rule a genuine chance to fire without abandoning its own logic.** Exit condition held fixed at RSI back above 50 for all four, matching the question of whether the ORIGINAL rule's exit still makes sense on whatever new entries a variant produces. "Episodes" below means distinct entry opportunities (the first day of a qualifying streak, not every day within it - what a bot would actually act on once per streak).

- **A: SMA(20) instead of SMA(50), same RSI<30 entry.** 21 qualifying days, 5 distinct episodes. Real examples: NVDA 2024-07-09 (RSI 21.4, $131.40 vs SMA $126.62) and NVDA 2024-07-12 (RSI 22.2) both eventually exited at a LOSS (-8.04%/-6.69% over ~52 trading days) when RSI recovered; AVGO 2024-08-13 (RSI 17.8) and 2024-09-11 (RSI 29.9) both exited at a real GAIN (+19.09%/+17.59%); SMCI 2024-10-28 (RSI 17.1) never recovered above RSI 50 within 60 trading days at all. Of the 4 episodes that did get an RSI-recovery exit, only 2 (50%) showed a price gain by exit - average +5.49%, but that average hides real losers, and the "never recovered" case shows the exit can simply not fire for a long time.
- **B: RSI threshold 35 instead of 30, same SMA(50).** Zero qualifying days. (Expected: RSI<35 is a strict superset condition of RSI<30, and the original scan already found zero at 30 - this confirms 35 alone isn't the lever that matters.)
- **C: RSI threshold 40 instead of 30, same SMA(50).** 9 qualifying days, 5 distinct episodes - all 5 got an RSI-recovery exit within 60 trading days (faster than variant A too - average 13.8 trading days held, vs. A's 41.3), and 3 of 5 (60%) showed a price gain by exit (average +2.66%). Examples: AVGO 2024-09-23 and 2024-09-27 both gained (+7.57%/+7.77%) into the same October 2024 rally variant A also caught; GOOGL 2026-06-03 gained +3.90% over 9 days; AMZN 2026-06-03 and JPM 2025-02-25 both lost slightly (-2.33%/-3.61%) despite the RSI-recovery exit firing.
- **D: "Near SMA" (within 2%, above or below) instead of strictly above, same RSI<30/SMA(50).** Zero qualifying days - when RSI(14) genuinely crosses below 30 for these symbols, price isn't just slightly below SMA(50), it's typically several percent below (consistent with the original scan's closest miss, DIS, being off by $6.09 - about 4.6% of its own SMA at the time) - "near" doesn't rescue this variant either.

**Honest read of all four**: A and C both give the rule a real, if infrequent, chance to fire (roughly 1-2 genuine episodes per symbol per ~4 years in this sample), at the cost of loosening either the trend filter (A) or the oversold threshold (C). Neither shows a clean, obviously-working exit story on this small sample - both have real losing episodes even when the RSI-recovery exit does fire, and variant A had one episode where it didn't fire at all for 60 trading days. This is small-sample evidence, not a verdict - exactly the same "could be a coin flip once you subtract the spread" honesty the original edge expectation already carried, now with a few concrete real numbers behind it instead of reasoning alone.

**Decision made: keep SMA(50) unchanged, move `rsiEntryThreshold` from 30 to 40 - shipped as a new rule, `rsi_pullback_uptrend_v2`, not an edit to v1's own params** (`lib/trading/bot-rule.ts`), for the exact reason v1's own header comment already states: a parameter change must never silently reinterpret what an already-recorded run meant. `ACTIVE_RULE_ID`/`ACTIVE_RULE_PARAMS` (also new, in the same file) is the one explicit, grep-able pointer `lib/db/bot-runs.ts` reads for selection, monitoring, and every new `createBotRun` call - a future v3 needs exactly that one line changed, not a search-and-replace. v1 stays defined, untouched, and still fully valid for any bot run already recorded under it.

- **B and D were ruled out as structural dead ends, not close misses.** B (RSI<35) found zero qualifying days - expected, since RSI<35 is a strict subset of RSI<40's own condition, which itself barely fires (9 days). D ("within 2% of SMA either side") also found zero - when RSI(14) genuinely crosses below 30 for these symbols, price isn't marginally below SMA(50), it's typically several percent below (the original scan's closest miss, DIS, was off by $6.09 - about 4.6% of its own SMA at the time). Loosening the SMA proximity doesn't rescue this combination; the divergence when RSI(14) genuinely crosses 30 is too large for "near" to matter.
- **C was chosen over A on rule-shape and practicality grounds, explicitly not on the win-rate numbers.** C leaves SMA(50) - the rule's own trend-context definition - completely unchanged, only relaxing how oversold "oversold" needs to be; A instead redefines what counts as the medium-term trend itself (SMA(20) is a materially different, shorter-horizon filter), which changes the rule's own shape more than its threshold. C's exits also arrive faster in this sample (~14 trading days average vs. A's ~41), which matters directly for live/scheduled testing - a rule whose exits take ~2 months to resolve is harder to observe and iterate on than one resolving in ~2-3 weeks.
- **The win-rate figures (A: 50%, C: 60%) are explicitly NOT being treated as validation and must not be read that way going forward.** n=5 episodes per variant is noise, not evidence, in either direction - a coin flip run 5 times can easily land 50-60% "wins" with no real edge behind it. The real test is live performance, measured honestly through the exact `bot_runs`/stats-panel measurement layer already built for this - the same intent the original v1 design had from the start (see the edge-expectation note above). Revisit this decision using real trade counts, not this backtest, once enough live data exists.
- **Scheduling (the GitHub Actions workflow for the bot worker) was deliberately not touched in this round** - still not wired up, per its own standing, separate decision (see Next).

## Open questions / deferred

- **An equity curve (portfolio value over time) is not possible with what this app currently records, and needs a real decision about how to start.** The transactions ledger (`lib/db/schema.ts`'s `transactions` table) records every cash delta with a running `balanceAfterCents`, but never a snapshot of total equity (cash + market value) at a point in time - market value depends on a live quote, which was never persisted. Two real options, not just one:
  - **(a) Forward-only daily snapshots.** Add an `equity_snapshots` table (accountId, date, cashCents, totalMarketValueCents, totalEquityCents) and a job that writes one row per account per day. Needs a trigger: either a scheduled job (Vercel Cron hitting a new route, once this app is deployed and has cron available) that snapshots every account at a fixed time such as market close, or an opportunistic snapshot taken whenever a user visits the dashboard (no cron needed, but misses days nobody visits, leaving gaps). The chart itself is straightforward once the data exists - a line/area series, reusing the same lightweight-charts pattern already built for `StockChart`. The real cost is that **history only starts accumulating from the day this ships** - weeks or months of past activity are simply gone from the curve.
  - **(b) A one-time retroactive backfill.** Since every fill is already in `orders`/`transactions` with an exact timestamp and price, it's possible to reconstruct what was held on any past day and value it using Alpaca's historical daily closes (the same bars endpoint `fetchDailyBars`/`fetchDailyBarsForSymbols` already use) - replay the order history day by day, track cash and positions as of each day's close, mark to that day's close price. This gives immediate full history back to account creation instead of an empty chart that only fills in going forward. The tradeoffs: meaningfully more upfront engineering than (a) alone (a batch reconstruction job, careful handling of non-trading days and any symbol that's since been delisted, and every historical point is an EOD close, not a true intraday mark - the curve can't show what equity was mid-day, only at each day's close). It also doesn't replace (a): a backfill only produces history up to "now," so forward-only recording is still needed afterward to keep the curve current.
  - **Recommendation if this gets built**: do both together, not (a) alone. Given this app already has everything (b) needs (a complete, exact-timestamped order ledger and access to historical closes), starting the curve at an empty chart when a populated one is achievable in the same effort seems like the wrong default - the backfill is a bounded, one-time cost, not an ongoing one. Worth scoping as its own session rather than folding into a future chart tweak.

The real cost: an order placed at 5pm for "tomorrow's session" will very likely be swept away by the next scheduled tick (every 5 minutes), not preserved. Not treated as a bug - genuinely a different, simpler definition of "day order" than a real broker's - but worth flagging to a user who expects after-hours limit orders to survive to the next open, and worth reconsidering if that turns out to matter in practice.

## Gotchas discovered

- **The concurrency test needed a synchronized mock delay to be trustworthy.** A plain `Promise.all` of two concurrent order placements looked like it tested the row lock, but timing was unreliable: one call's async setup could fully finish (and commit) before the other even started, so the test sometimes passed even with the lock deliberately removed. Fixed by delaying the mocked quote fetch by a fixed amount so both calls clear their pre-transaction work in near-lockstep, forcing genuine overlap — then verified by removing the lock and confirming the test _actually_ failed 4/4 runs, and passed 4/4 with the lock restored.
- **`npm run verify` and `npm ci` catch different classes of failure.** `verify` never reinstalls dependencies, so a `package.json`/`package-lock.json` mismatch is invisible to it — it only surfaces in CI's `npm ci`. That's why the pre-push hook now also runs `npm ci --dry-run`.
- **A skill directory named `design` silently collided with a built-in CLI command** (`/design`). The Skill tool refused to invoke it with an explicit error rather than doing something confusing. Renamed the directory to `trading-ui-design`.
- **A color token named `base` collided with Tailwind's built-in `text-base` utility** (font-size: 1rem) — defining `--color-base` made `text-base` ambiguous between font-size and text-color. Added a separate `--color-on-fg` token for the one place text needed to match the background color, instead of overloading `--color-base`.
- **`.claude/agents/` and `.claude/skills/` need a full quit-and-reopen to be picked up if the directory didn't exist when the session started** — not just a new message, and not fixable by telling Claude to "restart." Both the `reviewer` agent and the `trading-ui-design` skill hit this; work-arounds (running the agent's instructions through a general-purpose agent) confirmed the content was correct while the registration lag was purely a session/watcher issue.
- **Joining two independent one-to-many children onto the same parent in one query fans out into their cross product.** `getPortfolio`'s single-round-trip query LEFT JOINs `positions` and a 20-row `orders` subquery onto `accounts` in the same SELECT — for an account with P positions and O orders, that's P×O rows back, not P+O. Harmless at this app's scale (a handful of positions, orders capped at 20), but it must be deduplicated in JS (by symbol, by order id) rather than trusted as one-row-per-position — the integration test with 2 positions and 2 orders is what would catch a regression here.
- **Next.js 16 deprecated the `middleware.ts` file convention in favor of `proxy.ts`.** `next build` prints an explicit warning with a codemod command (`npx @next/codemod@canary middleware-to-proxy .`) — ran it rather than hand-renaming, since it also renames the exported function (`middleware` → `proxy`), not just the file.
- **Signup and login were verified structurally, not end to end against the live Supabase project.** Confirmed via build + curl: `/dashboard` redirects to `/login` when signed out (307), `/login` and `/signup` render with the expected form fields, typecheck/tests/format/build all pass. NOT run: an actual `signUp`/`signInWithPassword` round trip against the real project — doing that from here would create a real, permanent row in that project's `auth.users` with no service-role key available to remove it afterward, which is exactly the kind of action on shared external state this project's own conventions say to check before taking. The user's manual click-through is what actually proves the live path works. **Update: the user has since completed this click-through and confirmed it all works** (see Current status) — this entry is kept as a record of how it was verified in two stages, not as an open item.
- **A component file that imports a Server Action can become untestable in the default unit suite**, even for logic that's otherwise pure. `OrderTicket.tsx` imports `placeTrade`, which imports `lib/db/client.ts`, which throws immediately if `DATABASE_URL` isn't set — so importing anything at all from `OrderTicket.tsx`, even just a plain string-building helper like `describeRejection`, crashed the test file before a single test ran. Fixed by moving `describeRejection` into `components/orderMessages.ts`, which imports only `lib/trading/*` and `MarketStatusBanner` (itself DB-free). The same pattern already existed for `MarketStatusBanner`'s `openMessage`/`closedMessage` and `Delta`'s `formatDeltaAmount` - export the pure text-building function so it stays testable without a DOM, but watch what THAT file transitively imports, too.
- **Changing `formatCents`'s output format (adding thousands separators) could have silently broken its own round-trip test** (`toCents(formatCents(x)) === x`), which exists specifically to guarantee no precision is lost between the two functions. Extending `toCents`'s grammar to also accept grouped input (not just relaxing the test) is what kept the existing test meaningful and passing unchanged — a reminder that when a function's contract changes, the fix is usually to update its counterpart's contract too, not to loosen the assertion checking them against each other.
- **The `server-only` package throws unconditionally under plain Vitest**, breaking every test file that transitively imports a guarded module - not a false positive, just a real gap between how Vitest resolves modules and how Next's webpack build does. Next's build applies a conditional swap (real guard for the client, no-op for the server) that Vitest has no equivalent for, so it always gets the real, always-throwing version. Fixed by aliasing `"server-only"` to a local no-op shim (`vitest.server-only-shim.ts`) in both Vitest configs - the same fix `next/jest` ships for exactly this reason, so this wasn't a one-off workaround.
- **`pkill -f "next start"` (and similar) does not reliably kill Windows `node.exe` processes started via an npm script**, in this Git Bash environment. A stale server from an earlier `npm run start` survived multiple later `pkill` calls across this session and kept silently answering `curl` requests on port 3000 - which looked exactly like a real bug (stale page title, `/` not redirecting) until `netstat -ano | grep :3000` showed the same PID had been LISTENING the whole time. Fixed by killing the exact PID with `taskkill //F //PID <pid> //T`. When a local verification result looks inexplicable right after a code change, check `netstat`/`tasklist` for a leftover listener before assuming the code is wrong.
- **Capping `lib/db/client.ts`'s connection pool to `max: 1` (for the serverless-correctness reasons above) silently broke the row-lock concurrency test's actual validity, without making it fail.** The test still passed 10/10 after that change - but only because `max: 1` means the two concurrent `placeMarketOrder` calls it runs could never actually have two connections open at once, so postgres.js serialized them client-side before either transaction reached Postgres. The _outcome_ the test asserts (exactly one order succeeds) is trivially true under that serialization too, so nothing failed - but the test was no longer exercising the `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` lock it claims to prove.
  - **Why it was invisible**: nothing about this shows up in a test report. `npm run test:integration` printed the same "10 passed" whether the row lock existed or not - the failure mode is a test that quietly stopped testing anything, not a test that errors. The only way to see it was to deliberately break the thing the test claims to protect (comment out `.for("update")`) and check whether the test noticed. It didn't, the first time.
  - **How it was caught**: during a pre-push review, by re-running exactly the empirical check the test's own comment already describes doing once, historically ("verified by removing the lock and confirming the test _actually_ failed 4/4 runs"). That check had never been redone after `max: 1` was introduced.
  - **The fix, in two layers**: (1) a `DB_POOL_MAX` env var - unset (so `max: 1`) in production, set to `5` only in `vitest.integration.setup.ts` - restores genuine two-connection concurrency for the test process without changing production behavior. (2) `lib/db/client.ts` exports `poolMax`, and the concurrency test itself now asserts `poolMax >= 2` _before_ running, with a message explaining exactly why a lower value would make it pass trivially - so if this regresses again (`DB_POOL_MAX` unset, lowered, or renamed), the test fails loudly with an explanation instead of passing silently. Verified in both directions again after adding this: temporarily set `DB_POOL_MAX=1` in the setup file → the precondition itself fails with the intended message; restored to `5` → passes.
  - **Also added**: `.claude/agents/reviewer.md` check 8, "config changes that could invalidate an existing test without failing it" - triggered by diffs touching connection pooling, concurrency limits, timeouts, caching, or environment-conditional behavior, instructing the reviewer to identify affected tests and re-run the same break-it-and-check verification, not just reason about whether it's probably fine.
  - **The general lesson**: a config change made for one good reason (serverless correctness) can silently invalidate a test written under different assumptions (needing real concurrency) without that test ever going red - "the suite is green" and "the suite is still testing what it says it's testing" are not the same claim, and a change to shared infrastructure code is exactly when to re-check the second one, not just the first. A precondition assertion is the durable fix; a one-time manual re-verification is not, since nothing forces it to happen again next time.
- **Vercel only accepts even-numbered Node LTS majors (20, 22, 24, ...) - odd majors (21, 23, 25, ...) are always "Current," never LTS, and Vercel rejects them as a deploy target outright.** `engines.node` had been set to `25.6.1` because that happened to be the version installed on the dev machine at the time, not because anyone checked it against what the deploy target actually accepts - the mismatch only surfaced when a real deploy failed. `engines.node` (and `.nvmrc`, which must match it) must be chosen by checking Node's own release schedule / Vercel's supported-versions doc, never by running `node --version` locally and copying the answer. `scripts/check-node-engine.ts` (wired into `npm run verify`) now catches this locally before a push: it fails loudly if `engines.node`'s major version is odd, with no version list to keep updated, since "even is LTS, odd never is" is Node's permanent policy, not a snapshot.
- **A script's own top-level env-file loading can run too late relative to its own imports, because ES module imports are hoisted above the rest of the file's code.** `scripts/sync-assets.ts` called `process.loadEnvFile(".env.local")` in its own body, but a static `import { syncAssets } from "../lib/db/assets"` above it is evaluated first regardless of source order - and that import chain reaches `lib/db/client.ts`'s module-level `if (!process.env.DATABASE_URL) throw ...` before the env file had loaded, so the script crashed with "Missing DATABASE_URL" every time despite the loading code being right there in the file. Fixed by deferring the import: `process.loadEnvFile` first, then `const { syncAssets } = await import("../lib/db/assets")` inside an async `main()` - a dynamic import is not hoisted, so it only runs once actually reached. Watch for this in any future script that both loads `.env.local` itself and imports something that reads `process.env` at module scope (not inside a function body) - `lib/market/alpaca.ts`'s functions read env vars lazily inside each function for exactly this reason, but `lib/db/client.ts` reads `DATABASE_URL` eagerly at the top of the module.
- **`core.autocrlf=true` (Git for Windows' default) plus no `endOfLine` setting in `.prettierrc` is a standing trap on this machine, unrelated to any specific change.** Checked-out files get CRLF on disk; Prettier defaults to expecting LF; `format:check` can start failing across the whole repo for files nobody touched, and it isn't tied to any particular git operation that was ever fully identified (a `.gitattributes` `eol=lf` rule was added but didn't reliably force LF back on this checkout path). Fixed durably with `"endOfLine": "auto"` in `.prettierrc`, which makes Prettier preserve whatever line ending is already in a file instead of asserting one - if `format:check` ever fails broadly again with no corresponding content change, check this before assuming something is actually broken.
- **A `lightweight-charts` v5 series option can look like it belongs on the price scale and fail at compile time, not at review time, if it doesn't.** `autoscaleInfoProvider` (pinning RSI's subpanel to a fixed 0-100 range) configures the price scale's own behavior but is actually passed to `addSeries`, not `chart.priceScale(id).applyOptions()` - TypeScript's structural typing caught the misplacement immediately (`DeepPartial<PriceScaleOptions>` has no such property), which is the good case; the risk is options that happen to type-check in the wrong place. When adding a new chart option, verify which object the library's own typings declare it on before assuming based on what it conceptually configures.
- **Alpaca's bars endpoint treats both `start` and `end` as inclusive bounds** - confirmed empirically (querying `end=T` and, separately, `start=T` both returned the bar timestamped exactly `T`), not assumed from docs. This is what let the completed-range and forming-range queries in `fetchBars` both return the exact bar sitting on their shared boundary instant, duplicating it on the chart - fixed by subtracting 1ms from the completed query's `end`. Worth remembering for any other Alpaca endpoint with a `start`/`end` pair: don't assume the usual half-open convention without checking.
- **A "hidden overlay price scale" (a second named price scale confined by `scaleMargins` + `visible: false`) confines a series' bars but not its `lastValueVisible` last-value badge.** This is what let volume's "606.89K" label bleed into the price panel's own coordinate space even though the volume bars themselves stayed correctly confined to their band - a real, confirmed limitation of that technique, not a `scaleMargins` tuning problem. lightweight-charts v5's real multi-pane API (`addSeries(type, options, paneIndex)`) doesn't have this problem, since the label lives in a genuinely separate pane rather than a differently-scaled region of the same one.
- **A quote's bid/ask being present and being valid are two different checks, and this codebase only ever made the first one.** Alpaca reports `0` for a side with no active quotation (confirmed the real cause of a real screenshot: Bid $295.94 / Ask $0.00 / Spread −$295.94 rendered while the market was closed) rather than omitting the field or erroring - so `bp: 0`/`ap: 0` parses as a perfectly valid `z.number()` and sails straight through Zod's boundary validation. Every render site in this app already had a "no quote for this symbol" branch (`bidCents === null || askCents === null`), but that branch only fires for a symbol Alpaca omits entirely - a zero-priced side isn't null, so it skipped every one of those checks and reached the display and the spread math as if it were a real price. Fixed once, at the actual boundary (`fetchQuote`/`fetchQuotes` in `lib/market/alpaca.ts`, backed by `isValidTwoSidedQuote` in `lib/trading/quote.ts`), rather than patching each render site separately - a zero-priced side is now treated identically to a missing quote before it ever leaves that file. Watch for this in any future Alpaca (or other external) integration: a schema type check (`z.number()`) proves a field parses, not that its value is meaningful - "present" and "valid" need a separate check whenever an API can legitimately return a sentinel value (0, empty string, etc.) for "no data," and a boundary that only checks presence will let that sentinel through as if it were real.
- **The same missing-quote gap was more than a display bug - it reached the actual order-fill path.** `placeMarketOrder` (`lib/db/orders.ts`) prices a real fill from the same `fetchQuote` call, and before this was fixed, a zero-priced ask/bid could have filled a buy/sell at $0.00 the moment it occurred, not just rendered oddly - and the existing `market_closed` rejection in `executeMarketOrder` doesn't cover this case, since Alpaca can report a zero-priced side during market hours too (thin liquidity), not only while closed. Found by deliberately checking "does this same bug reach anywhere money actually moves," not just the places it was originally reported rendering - the display bug and the execution-path risk turned out to share one root cause and one fix, but only because the execution path was checked at all.
- **Rewriting `lib/market/alpaca.test.ts` from scratch (via `Write`, without reading what was already there) silently deleted a real, unrelated `fetchQuotes` test suite** that had nothing to do with the change being made. Caught by reviewing `git status`/`git diff` before committing, not by any test failure - the suite still passed with the file replaced, since nothing else exercised those deleted cases. Restored the original tests and merged the new ones in alongside them. The `Write` tool requires reading a file first specifically to make this mistake harder, but reading doesn't prevent overwriting; it's what you do with the read content when composing the replacement that either preserves or destroys it. Prefer `Edit` (append/insert) over `Write` (full-file replace) for extending an existing test file with new, unrelated cases.
- **A plain `fetch()` to a Route Handler does not get the automatic client Router Cache invalidation a Server Action gets for free.** `placeTrade` (a Server Action) calling `revalidatePath` inside it is enough on its own to make the calling client re-fetch fresh data - that's part of the Server Actions protocol. `placeLimitOrder`/`cancelOrder`'s Route Handlers also call `revalidatePath`, but since `OrderTicket`/`PendingOrdersPanel` reach them via a plain client-side `fetch`, that only marks the server-side cache stale - nothing tells the browser to refetch. Missed on the first pass (copied the "no `router.refresh()` needed" reasoning from the existing Server-Action-based flow without re-checking whether it still applied to a different call mechanism), caught by reasoning through what actually wires a Route Handler's `revalidatePath` back to the calling client, not by a test. Fixed by calling `router.refresh()` explicitly after every successful limit-order placement/cancellation. Worth remembering for any future Route-Handler-first feature in this app: `revalidatePath` inside the route is necessary but not sufficient when the caller is a plain `fetch`, unlike when the caller is a Server Action.
- **Two operations that each lock a row must always lock in the same order, or two concurrent operations locking the "same two things" in opposite orders can deadlock.** `placeLimitOrder` locks only the account row; `cancelOrder` locks only the order row; the worker's fill path locks the order row first, then (only if it's actually going to fill) the account row - never the reverse, and never both at once from any other path. Documented here as an invariant to preserve, not just a fact about the current code: a future change that makes any operation lock an account, then reach for a specific order's lock (or vice versa, in a different order than the worker already established) would reintroduce a real deadlock risk between the worker and whatever that new path is, not something a quick test run would necessarily catch under normal load.
- **A "don't touch the real production schema without asking" policy protects against the wrong half of the risk if code that depends on the new schema still gets deployed anyway.** This exact thing happened: the limit-orders round's code went out (pushed, and the app auto-deployed - apparently already live at `trading-sim-sigma.vercel.app`, which this log hadn't even been tracking) while the schema push was deliberately withheld pending the user's go-ahead, and the result was a real 500 on the live `/dashboard` - `getLastLimitOrderWorkerRun()` querying a table that only existed locally. Being cautious about the database half of a change while shipping the code half anyway doesn't avoid the risk, it just moves where it surfaces - a real user hitting a real error, instead of a schema push landing when it wasn't wanted. Worth asking about BOTH halves together next time a feature needs new schema, framed as one decision ("this deploy needs a matching schema push, in the same conversation, or the code shouldn't go out yet") rather than treating "hold off on the database" as the safe default in isolation.
- **The real Supabase database still cannot take a plain `drizzle-kit push`, confirmed again on a second, unrelated schema change** (adding `limit_order_worker_runs` + the `order_status` enum's `"expired"` value) - the exact same crash (`checkValue.replace` on `undefined`) as the original `drizzle-team/drizzle-orm#4496` finding in `drizzle/0001_add_assets_and_sync_runs.sql`. This is a property of the real database's own constraint representation (something Supabase-specific confuses drizzle-kit's introspection), not of any particular schema change - every future real-database migration should expect to need `scripts/apply-migration.ts` and a hand-written SQL file, not attempt `db:push` first and only fall back once it fails. `ALTER TYPE ... ADD VALUE` for a new enum value and unrelated `CREATE TYPE`/`CREATE TABLE` statements combine fine in one transaction via that script, as long as the new enum value is never itself referenced later in that same transaction (Postgres forbids using a value added earlier in an uncommitted transaction) - this migration's own comment documents why it's safe here.
- **A floating dropdown with no explicit height cap will grow to fit however many results come back, and in a dense panel layout that means overlapping whatever's below it, not just looking tall.** `SymbolAutocomplete`'s first version had no `max-height`/`overflow-y` on its results list - fine for a handful of matches, but a real query ("INTEL," 20 results before the `searchAssets` ranking fix even mattered) grew tall enough to cover the dashboard's Positions table and the pending-orders staleness banner underneath it, confirmed from a real screenshot, not caught by anything automated (this app still has no rendered-DOM test setup to catch a layout overlap either way). Fixed with a capped `max-h-72 overflow-y-auto`, plus an explicit `scrollIntoView` on the highlighted row when it changes via keyboard - without that second part, capping the height alone would have silently broken keyboard navigation past the visible rows (the highlight would move somewhere the user can't see). Any future floating/absolutely-positioned list in this app (another autocomplete, a context menu) should assume it needs both from the start, not add them reactively after someone notices the overlap.
- **Calling an existing DB-layer function as a black box inside a new transaction is a real nesting risk, not just a style preference, once that function opens its own transaction internally.** The bot's entry flow needed to place both a market buy and a resting limit sell atomically alongside a bot-run row lock; `placeMarketOrder`/`placeLimitOrder` each already open and manage their own `db.transaction`, so calling either from inside `tryEnterBotRun`'s own transaction would have held two pooled connections open for one logical operation, not one - fine at this app's tiny scale, but exactly the kind of thing that stops working the moment the connection pool is small (see `DB_POOL_MAX` in `lib/db/client.ts`). Fixed by reusing the shared _sub_-helpers those functions themselves are built from (`applyFillEffects`/`recordFillTransaction`, and a new accountId-scoped `cancelOrderByAccountId` extracted from `cancelOrder`) rather than the top-level functions - the money math still isn't duplicated, but the transaction boundary stays singular. Worth checking for this specifically before calling any existing `lib/db/*.ts` function from inside a new transaction in this codebase: does it open its own `db.transaction`, and if so, is there a lower-level shared piece to call instead.
- **Mocking a module wholesale for a test (`vi.mock(path, () => ({...}))`) replaces every export, including ones the test file never calls directly but that the code under test's own transitive imports still need at module-evaluation time.** `lib/db/bot-runs.test.ts` mocked `lib/trading/indicators` to control `rsi`/`sma` without feeding a real price series - but `lib/trading/bot-rule.ts` also imports that module's `DEFAULT_RSI_PERIOD` as a top-level constant (`RSI_PULLBACK_UPTREND_V1_PARAMS`'s own initializer), which isn't a function call the mock could lazily supply - it's read once, immediately, when the module loads. Left out of the mock's returned object, this crashed with "No DEFAULT_RSI_PERIOD export is defined" before a single test ran. Worth checking what ELSE a module exports - and whether anything in the dependency chain reads it at module-load time rather than call time - before mocking it wholesale, not just what the test itself calls.
- **The `text-base` / `--color-base` collision already documented in this file (see the earlier entry above) is a live trap, not a one-time historical incident - it bit again, in a different file, and rendered a real element invisible rather than just failing to compile.** `WatchlistStar.tsx` used `text-base` purely for its font-size meaning (sizing the star glyph to 1rem), with no awareness that the same class name is also a color utility once `--color-base` exists in the theme - there's no lint rule or type error that catches this, since both interpretations are valid Tailwind, so it silently rendered the star in the page-background color with no warning of any kind. The general lesson stands exactly as originally written ("one meaning silently wins"), but this specific recurrence sharpens it: **any future use of `text-base` anywhere in this codebase for its font-size meaning is a latent bug, not just a stylistic risk** - grep for `\btext-base\b` before adding new markup, and prefer `text-[1rem]` (or another explicit non-colliding value) instead of ever reaching for the built-in name again. **Swept the whole codebase for this class after fixing WatchlistStar, specifically to close it as a class rather than wait for the third instance to get caught by a screenshot**: a plain-text grep for `text-base` across every file, a `.tsx`-scoped word-boundary grep to rule out partial-name false matches, and a separate check for dynamic class construction (a template literal building `text-${...}` from a variable that could resolve to `"base"`) all came back with nothing beyond WatchlistStar's own now-fixed usage and this file's/the design skill's own prose describing the collision. **Result: no further instances found** - the two known cases (the design skill's own inverted-button example, caught before shipping, and WatchlistStar, caught after) remain the only two real occurrences this collision has ever actually caused in this codebase.
- **When hand-parametrized fixture-hunting keeps failing across many structurally different shapes, the fix is direct numerical optimization against the real function, not more manual shapes.** Building a real (unmocked) `rsi()`/`sma()` fixture satisfying RSI(14)<30 AND close>SMA(50) took, in order: a 1000-day real-data scan across 18 symbols (zero matches out of 436 real oversold days), then roughly a dozen distinct hand-authored synthetic price-path families (smooth uptrends, choppy uptrends, old-dip-then-recovery, front-loaded crashes, plateau-then-dip, single-day crashes) swept across hundreds of thousands of parameter combinations total, all failing - close, but never crossing both thresholds at once. What actually worked: a plain hill-climbing search directly over the 50-value price array itself (perturb one random day, keep the change only if it improves a combined "how far past both thresholds" score, restart from a fresh random seed if stuck), which found a genuine solution on effectively the first attempt. The lesson: for a two-constraint numeric target against a real, already-correct function, searching the function's own input space directly is a fundamentally more powerful tool than reasoning about "what shape of input would satisfy this" by hand - reach for it sooner, not as a last resort after manual guessing is exhausted.
